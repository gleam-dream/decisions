# An occurrence may carry its own deadline

<a id="adr-0054"></a>

Recorded on 2026-11-17. Extends [ADR-0008](0008-time-and-the-record-decide-never-the-delivery-of-a-job.md#adr-0008) and [ADR-0050](0050-a-trigger-is-admitted-before-the-model-runs.md#adr-0050).

## Decision

- An application may give one occurrence a deadline of its own, an instant it knows when it admits the trigger: `decisions.expect_until(.., valid_until:)` on the expectation, and `decisions.valid_until(proposal, at:)` on the proposal. `expect` and a proposal without it set none. The desk rounds a given instant down to whole milliseconds, as it does the store clock.
- The deadline is written on the record when it is set. The `expected` entry carries it as `valid_until`, left out of the stored text when there is none. The `recommended` entry's `valid_until` holds the **effective deadline**: the earliest of the point's validity counted from the recommendation, the record's deadline, and the proposal's. A repeated expectation with an earlier deadline appends another `expected` entry, and the record's deadline is the earliest of them; one with an equal or later deadline, or with none, writes nothing. A deadline is never moved later.
- A record not begun that carries a deadline is due at it: its head's `due` is the deadline, and every write to such a record compares its wake times (`occurrence.pending_wake_times`) before and after and calls the port's `schedule` when they differ, so one wake-up is pending at the deadline, and none once the record ended. At or after the deadline by the store clock the record is **lapsed**, whether or not that wake-up was delivered. `wake` then appends `expired` with cause `DeadlinePassed` and admits one settlement notice; the head's status becomes `expired`, and the record is settled. `happened` on a lapsed record records `DeadlinePassed` instead of the event, as it does for a lapsed occurrence, and reports the key in `not_begun`. `expect_until` on an empty key with a deadline already passed writes the expectation and its expiry at once, so the admitting transaction can still commit.
- `expect`, `expect_until`, `record_failure` and `record_failure_with` on a lapsed record write nothing and are refused with `DeadlinePassedBefore(deadline)`, a new refusal whose disposition is `Final`. So are `read` and every verb that needs an occurrence. `read_record` still returns `NotBegun`, with the cause in `Pending.ended` (formerly `superseded`, renamed because it now holds a deadline as well as an event).
- A recommendation whose effective deadline has passed when it is admitted, through `recommend` or `recommend_once`, is recorded and expires at once with `DeadlinePassed`: no policy runs, and nothing is shown or released. `recommend_once` returns that expired occurrence, and `isolated.recommend` returns it as `Recommended(occurrence)`, as both already do for a key recommended earlier and expired since. On a lapsed record whose expiry is not yet written, the desk writes the expiry first and the recommendation after it, so a lapsed record has one shape whichever comes first, the wake-up or the recommendation: `expected`, `expired`, `recommended`. The fold lets a `recommended` entry follow an `expired` entry whose cause is `DeadlinePassed`, and the occurrence starts `Expired(DeadlinePassed)`. One settlement notice is admitted for the record, by whichever write ended it; the recommendation that follows an expiry admits none. Its account is `Lapsed(DeadlinePassed)`. After a supersession nothing is recommended, as before.
- One expiry path: a deadline of the occurrence's own and one from the point's validity fold into one deadline, with one wake-up and one cause; once recommended, the same refusals apply (`NotAwaiting` from `open`, `resume`, `note`, `expand`, `decide`, `inform`). Queues order by the head's `due`, which is the effective deadline.
- The storage port and the PostgreSQL schema do not change: the deadline lives in the entries, and the head's `due` already holds a time for any status. The conformance suite gains one check that an adapter keeps a pending head's due time and its wake-up.
- Moving a deadline later is not built.

## What changes for consumers

- New: `decisions.expect_until`, `decisions.valid_until`, `decisions.DeadlinePassedBefore`, `occurrence.pending_wake_times`, `occurrence.pending_has_lapsed`, `isolated.Lapsed`.
- Source changes: `entry.Expected` and `occurrence.Pending` gain a `valid_until` field, so a pattern that names every field, or a constructor, must add it. `Pending.superseded` is renamed `ended`. `learning.Coverage` gains `lapsed_before`. `decisions.Woken` gains `LapsedBefore(pending)`, the answer of `wake` that expired a record not begun; `waker` treats it as done. `isolated.Attempted` gains `Lapsed(deadline)`, returned when `record_failure` is refused as lapsed, and is documented, like `Refusal`, as growing in a minor release.
- Behaviour: none for a key without a deadline; its stored entries are the same bytes as before. With one, `recommend_once` and `isolated.recommend` may return an occurrence already expired, as a redelivery could before, and a follower may be handed a record lapsed before it began, which it reads as `DeadlinePassedBefore` and drops like a superseded one.

## Rationale and alternatives

- dash-crm, Dash's back office answering customers by WhatsApp, drafts each reply as an occurrence of its point `reply.draft`. A reply may be sent only until 24 hours after the customer's latest message. A point's validity counts from the recommendation, so with a 24-hour validity a draft recommended 90 seconds after the message would expire 90 seconds after the window closes, and an expected record whose model keeps failing would never expire at all.
- Computing the deadline at read from a field the application keeps was rejected: the record would no longer say when the occurrence could be decided, and a replay "as known at T" could not tell it.
- A second expiry path, such as a job the application schedules itself, was rejected: it would decide by the delivery of a job, which ADR-0008 forbids.
- A late recommendation, refused or recorded: ADR-0008 requires one answer whether or not the expiry wake-up came first. Refusing would keep the record shorter and match `SupersededBefore`, but lose the late output. The owner ruled to keep it on the record: the output the model produced for the trigger, and when, is evidence of how slow it was and of what it would have said. A late failed attempt is refused: it adds nothing the expiry does not already show. A superseded record still refuses its recommendation: that output answered a trigger the world has since replaced, so it is not an example of the point's work.
- What a settlement follower is handed depends on when the notice is delivered: the expired occurrence, or, when the notice is delivered before a late recommendation, the lapsed record not begun. The notice says that the record settled, not that it is complete; whoever learns from late outputs reads the records, not the notices.
- Adding a labelled argument to `expect` was rejected: every existing caller would change. `expect_until` follows `record_failure_with`.
- A limit: on PostgreSQL every expected key with a deadline submits a Grind job, which Grind cannot cancel. When the model answers in seconds, `schedule` removes the wake-up row and the job finds nothing to do when it runs at the deadline: one job per expected key.

## Evidence and history

- dash-crm's request for its point `reply.draft`, and the owner's rulings of 2026-11-17: an occurrence may carry its own deadline; a recommendation after that deadline is recorded and expired ("keeping it on record"); `record_failure` and `expect` after it are refused.
- Tracker issue 30 of `issues/first-applications-findings`, which holds the build and its tests.
